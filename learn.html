Common conditions

What's actually causing the pain — explained without jargon, with in-depth guides from Atlantic Spine Center's clinical library.

Herniated Disc

When the soft center of a spinal disc pushes through its outer wall, it can press on nearby nerves — causing back pain, leg pain, numbness, or weakness. Most cases improve without surgery.

Read the full guide →

Sciatica

Pain that radiates from the lower back down the leg, following the path of the sciatic nerve. Usually a symptom of something else — often a herniated disc or stenosis — not a diagnosis by itself.

Read the full guide →

Spinal Stenosis

A narrowing of the spinal canal that squeezes the nerves inside, most common after 50. The hallmark: pain or heaviness in the legs when walking that eases when you sit or lean forward.

Read the full guide →

Degenerative Disc Disease

Not actually a disease — the natural wearing of spinal discs over time. For some it causes chronic pain and stiffness. Managing it well is about function, not just imaging findings.

Read the full guide →

Lower Back Pain

The most common reason adults see a spine specialist. Most episodes resolve within weeks — the key is knowing which warning signs mean it's time to be evaluated.

Read the full guide →

Pinched Nerve

Pressure on a nerve root — from a disc, bone spur, or narrowed canal — that causes pain, tingling, or weakness along that nerve's path. Where you feel it points to where it's pinched.

Read the full guide →

Interventional Pain Management

Image-guided, non-opioid treatments — targeted injections, nerve blocks, radiofrequency ablation — that treat the source of pain rather than masking it.

Endoscopic Spine Surgery

When surgery is the right answer, it doesn't have to be open surgery. Endoscopic techniques use an incision smaller than a dime — typically with less tissue damage and faster recovery.

Learn the technique →

Fusion alternative procedures

Fusion is sometimes the right operation — but it permanently joins vertebrae and changes how your spine moves. If you've been told you need one, it's worth understanding the motion-preserving alternatives before you decide.

Endoscopic Discectomy

For many herniated discs, removing just the fragment pressing on the nerve — through an incision smaller than a dime — relieves pain while keeping the disc and its motion intact.

Read the full guide →

Artificial Disc Replacement

Instead of fusing two vertebrae together, a worn cervical disc can be replaced with an artificial one that preserves natural motion — often reducing stress on the discs above and below.

Read the full guide →

When Fusion Is Still Right

Some conditions genuinely call for fusion. Even then, smaller is possible: mini-fusion techniques stabilize the spine through smaller incisions with less muscle disruption than traditional open fusion.

Prevention, our first word

The least invasive treatment of all is the problem you never develop. Education on staying ahead of injury and chronic disease — and where newer tools fit in. Every topic below is a conversation to have with a physician, not a substitute for one.

Preventing Back Injury

Most back injuries trace to a few preventable patterns: lifting with a rounded back, weak core and hip muscles, long uninterrupted sitting, and bone health left unchecked. Strength work, lifting mechanics, and movement breaks meaningfully lower the risk.

Talk to a physician →

GLP-1s: Obesity & Metabolic Health

GLP-1 medications mimic a natural gut hormone that regulates appetite and blood sugar. For many people with obesity or type 2 diabetes they produce substantial, sustained improvement — and work best alongside nutrition, strength training, and physician supervision.

Talk to a physician →

GLP-1s & Heart Health

In large clinical trials, GLP-1 therapy has reduced the risk of heart attack and stroke in adults with cardiovascular disease and excess weight — one reason these medications are increasingly part of preventive care, not just weight management.

Talk to a physician →

GLP-1s & Brain Health

Researchers are studying whether GLP-1s protect the brain, since diabetes and obesity are known dementia risk factors. The evidence is still emerging — early results are mixed — but managing metabolic health remains one of the best-supported ways to protect long-term cognition.

Questions patients ask us first

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

In most cases, no. You can book directly with any practice in the network. Some insurance plans require a referral for specialist visits, so it is worth a quick check with your plan first.

Does back pain always need surgery?

No — the large majority of back and neck pain resolves without surgery. The network's practices treat conservatively first: diagnosis, targeted non-surgical care, and surgery only when it is clearly the right tool for a clearly identified problem.

Which practice will actually treat me?

Always the independent medical practice you choose, such as Atlantic Spine Center. Praesidio Health provides the operations and technology behind the scenes; your care, your records, and your relationship stay with your doctors.

Do you take my insurance?

Each practice manages its own insurance participation. When you book, the practice's team will verify your coverage before your visit so there are no surprises.

What is a second opinion worth?

A great deal — especially before spine surgery. If you have been told you need an operation, an independent review of your imaging and history can confirm the plan or surface a less invasive alternative.

Is Praesidio Health a hospital system?

No. We are a physician-led enterprise that backs independent practices with enterprise-grade operations and technology, so you get the strength of a system with the personal care of an independent doctor.

Ready to talk to a physician?

Education is the starting point. A diagnosis from a specialist who answers to you — not a hospital system — is the next step.
